Reflection

What I learned. My OJT gave me a much closer look at how visual communication supports real work — turning a clinical protocol or a public-health message into something a reader can understand in seconds. I learned to think about hierarchy, spacing, and color choices not as decoration, but as tools for making information easier to trust and act on.

Skills I developed. I became a lot more comfortable working in Canva under real deadlines, building layouts from a blank canvas, and keeping a consistent visual style across a series of related materials. I also picked up habits I didn't have before: asking for the correct reference material before designing, requesting feedback early, and revising instead of starting over.

My favorite output. The infographic series was the work I enjoyed most, since it asked me to simplify a full page of clinical text into a handful of visuals that still had to be accurate. Seeing a finished design printed and posted for actual use made the effort feel real.

Challenges I encountered. Balancing accuracy with simplicity was the hardest part — a poster is only useful if it's both correct and quick to read, and getting both right usually took more than one revision. Working within the host office's existing branding while still keeping each piece clear also took some trial and error.

Overall experience. This training period pushed me to grow beyond just following instructions — it taught me to ask better questions, manage my own output, and take pride in work other people would actually use. I'm leaving this OJT with a stronger portfolio and a clearer sense of the kind of work I want to keep doing.
